How much electricity and carbon is behind a week of Claude Code?

This page estimates the data-centre electricity (kWh) and greenhouse gas emissions (kg CO2e) behind Claude Code use. You can start from a full week of a subscription plan or from your own token counts. By default it shows a full week of the Max plan (formerly Max 5x) used to its limit. Every result is a range: a low, a central best guess and a high value.

⚠︎ How uncertain this is

  • Nobody outside Anthropic knows how much energy a Claude token takes. Anthropic publishes no energy figures per token, prompt or plan, so every figure here is inferred from other companies’ disclosures, independent estimates for other models, or Anthropic’s API price ratios.
  • Nobody outside Anthropic knows how many tokens a plan allows. Limits are published only as multiples of Pro. The token counts come from two commercial sites’ measurements, one of them a single user.
  • Low and high are bounds, not a confidence interval. “Low” sets every parameter to its low value at once, and “high” sets every one high, so the spread is very wide (about 190 times for a week of Max). The central value could easily be wrong by a factor of 3 either way.
  • The biggest unknown is the energy cost of a cache read. About 96% of Claude Code tokens are cache reads, and the evidence on their cost is a price ratio, not a measurement.

How this works

  1. Count the tokens by type. For a plan, take the estimated tokens in a full week, multiply by the share of the plan you use, and split them into fresh input, cache writes, cache reads and output using the token mix. If you enter your own counts, they are used as they are.
  2. Server energy. Multiply the tokens of each type (in millions) by the energy per million tokens of that type, then by the model-size multiplier, and add them up. This is the energy of the servers: accelerators, host CPUs and memory, and a share of idle capacity.
  3. Data-centre electricity. Multiply by the PUE (power usage effectiveness) to add cooling and other facility overhead.
  4. Emissions. Multiply the electricity by the grid’s carbon intensity.
  5. Embodied emissions (off by default) add 42 g CO2e per kWh for making the hardware, a ratio derived from Google’s figures (S4).

Claude Code sends the whole conversation back to the model on every turn. Anthropic’s prompt cache keeps the model’s working state for the earlier part, so most tokens on each call are cache reads. A cache read skips most of the computation, so it costs much less per token than fresh input, but because there are so many of them they are still the largest share of the energy. Output tokens cost the most per token, because each one needs a full pass through the model.

Where the range comes from. The low result sets every parameter to its low value at once, and the high result sets every one high. These are bounds rather than a statistical interval: it is unlikely that every parameter is at its extreme together. If you set a parameter yourself, the low, central and high results all use your value for it. The “what drives the range” table shows which assumption matters most. For a typical Claude Code mix it is the energy of a cache read.

Location-based or market-based carbon? Location-based emissions use the average carbon intensity of the grid where the electricity is used. Market-based emissions subtract the renewable electricity a company has contracted for. Google’s 2024 figures show the gap: 345 g CO2e/kWh location-based against 94 market-based (S4). Market-based figures show what the company has paid for. Location-based figures are closer to the physical effect of extra demand on today’s grid. The central value here is location-based (the US average, 350), and the market-based figure is the low end.

Not included: training the model (Watershed, S9, puts it at 32% to 56% of a model’s lifetime footprint), networking, your own computer, water use, and embodied emissions unless you switch them on.
